House extension cost UK: a realistic 2026 budget guide

Almost every house extension conversation eventually becomes a budgeting conversation — and almost every budget conversation underestimates the real total, because the headline build cost is only part of the bill.

What actually drives the price per square metre

The single biggest driver isn't the size of the extension — it's complexity. A simple single-storey box extension costs meaningfully less per square metre than a double-storey build, a wraparound extension with multiple structural openings, or anything involving significant groundwork, structural steel, or matching an unusual existing roofline. Finish level matters just as much: a basic finish and a high-spec kitchen-diner with bifold doors, underfloor heating and a vaulted ceiling can differ enormously in cost for an identical footprint.

Single-storey vs double-storey vs wraparound

A single-storey rear extension is generally the most cost-efficient way to add space per square metre, since you're not paying for a second storey of foundations, structure and roofing. A double-storey extension costs more in total but is often more cost-efficient per square metre of new space than two separate single-storey builds, because you share one set of foundations and one roof across both floors. Wraparound extensions (side plus rear combined) tend to sit at the more expensive end, due to the additional structural junctions and roof detailing involved.

The hidden costs people forget to budget for

Planning permission or building regulations approval, party wall agreements with neighbours where applicable, structural engineer fees, temporary living arrangements or storage during the build, and VAT on labour and materials (extensions don't usually qualify for the reduced or zero VAT rates that apply to some other property work) can add a meaningful percentage on top of the headline build quote. None of these show up in a rough "£ per square metre" estimate, which is exactly why they catch people out.

Not every extension needs full planning permission — many fall under Permitted Development rights, subject to size and boundary limits — but building regulations approval is a separate, near-universal requirement regardless of the planning route.

Budgeting sensibly

Get at least three like-for-like quotes covering the same specification, and build in a contingency — unexpected groundwork, drainage issues or existing structural surprises are common once walls come down, not the exception.
