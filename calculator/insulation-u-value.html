Insulation U-Value Calculator

Estimate the U-value of a wall or roof build-up.

How it works

A U-value measures how much heat passes through a building element (wall, roof, floor) — the lower the U-value, the better insulated it is. It's calculated as the reciprocal of total thermal resistance (R-value): each layer in a build-up (existing structure, insulation, surface films) has its own resistance, and these add together before being inverted to get the final U-value.

UK context

Insulation material conductivity (how easily heat passes through it, measured in W/mK) varies significantly by material — some rigid foam insulants have very low conductivity (meaning better performance for a given thickness) compared with materials like mineral wool, which typically needs to be thicker to achieve the same resistance. Building Regulations set minimum U-value requirements for different elements in new builds and major renovations, which is why getting this calculation right matters for compliance, not just comfort.

Tips

  • Check your specific insulation product's stated thermal conductivity (lambda value) on its data sheet rather than assuming a generic figure — this varies meaningfully between products.
  • This simplified calculator handles a single insulation layer — real build-ups often have multiple layers (e.g. insulation plus a cavity plus plasterboard), each contributing its own resistance.
  • For actual Building Regulations compliance (rather than a general estimate), use a full calculation from your insulation manufacturer or a qualified assessor.

Frequently asked questions

What does a U-value actually measure?

It measures the rate of heat transfer through a building element — expressed in W/m²K. A lower U-value means less heat escapes, so lower is better for insulation performance.

How is U-value related to R-value?

U-value is the mathematical reciprocal of total thermal resistance (R-value): U = 1 / R. Adding more insulation increases R-value, which correspondingly reduces (improves) the U-value.

Does insulation thickness alone determine the U-value?

No — both thickness and the material's thermal conductivity matter. A thinner layer of a very low-conductivity material can achieve the same resistance as a much thicker layer of a higher-conductivity material.

A quick note

This is a simplified single-layer estimate with a standard surface resistance allowance. For actual Building Regulations compliance, use a full calculation from your insulation manufacturer or a qualified assessor.
