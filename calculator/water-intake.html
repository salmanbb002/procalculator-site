Daily Water Intake Calculator

Estimate how much water you should drink per day.

What is the Daily Water Intake Calculator?

A daily water intake calculator gives a general guideline figure for how much water you might need per day, based on your body weight and activity level. It works by multiplying your weight in kilograms by a millilitres-per-kilogram figure that increases with activity level (roughly 30ml/kg for low activity, up to 40ml/kg for high activity), then converting the total to litres and an approximate number of 250ml glasses. To calculate it manually: bodyweight (kg) × 30-40 (depending on activity), divided by 1,000 for litres — this is a general guideline rather than personalised medical advice, since real needs vary with climate, health conditions, pregnancy or breastfeeding, so consult a healthcare professional for specific guidance.

How it works

This uses a commonly cited general guideline of roughly 30-40ml of water per kilogram of body weight per day, adjusted upward for higher activity levels (since exercise increases fluid loss through sweat). It's a population-level rule of thumb rather than a precise physiological calculation, and it's presented here in that spirit.

UK context

Total daily fluid needs actually include water from food and other drinks, not just what you drink as plain water — and needs genuinely vary a great deal based on climate, health conditions, pregnancy or breastfeeding, and individual factors that a simple weight-based formula can't capture.

Tips

  • Hot weather, high humidity, and intense or prolonged exercise all significantly increase fluid needs beyond a baseline guideline figure.
  • Urine colour is a commonly used practical hydration indicator — pale yellow generally suggests good hydration, while consistently dark urine can suggest you need more fluids (though certain vitamins and medications can also affect colour).
  • If you have a health condition affecting fluid balance (kidney, heart or certain other conditions), general hydration guidelines may not apply — follow your healthcare provider's specific advice instead.

Frequently asked questions

Is 'drink 8 glasses a day' an accurate rule?

It's a popular rule of thumb, but actual needs vary a lot by body size, activity level, climate and individual factors — a weight-based estimate (like the one this calculator uses) is generally considered a more personalised starting point, though still a general guideline rather than an exact figure.

Does this include water from food and other drinks?

This estimate is for total fluid intake as a guideline — in practice, a meaningful portion of daily hydration comes from food and other beverages, not just plain water, which this simple calculator doesn't separately break down.

A quick note

A general guideline, not personalised medical advice. Needs vary with climate, health conditions, pregnancy/breastfeeding and individual factors — consult a healthcare professional for specific guidance.
