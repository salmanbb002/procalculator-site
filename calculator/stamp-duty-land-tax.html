Stamp Duty (SDLT) Calculator

New

Estimate Stamp Duty Land Tax on residential property purchases.

How it works

Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) in England and Northern Ireland is calculated using a banded system — like Income Tax, you pay increasing rates only on the portion of the price that falls within each band, not the whole purchase price at the top rate. Buying a property for £350,000 doesn't mean the whole amount is taxed at the same rate; the first slice is tax-free, the next slice is taxed at a lower rate, and so on.

UK context

First-time buyers get relief in the form of a higher nil-rate threshold, provided the purchase price is below a set ceiling — above that ceiling, no relief applies and standard rates are used on the whole price. Buying an additional residential property (a second home or buy-to-let) generally adds a surcharge on top of the standard bands, applied to the full purchase price. Scotland uses its own system (Land and Buildings Transaction Tax, LBTT) and Wales uses Land Transaction Tax (LTT) — both structured similarly to SDLT but with different bands and rates, so this England/NI calculator doesn't apply there.

Tips

  • SDLT is due within a set number of days of completion — your conveyancing solicitor normally handles the calculation and payment as part of the purchase process.
  • If you're not sure whether you qualify as a first-time buyer (for example, if you've inherited a share of a property before), check gov.uk's specific eligibility rules rather than assuming.
  • The additional-property surcharge applies even if you're replacing your main residence but haven't sold the old one yet by completion — though a refund may be claimable once it's sold within the required window.

Frequently asked questions

How is SDLT actually calculated?

SDLT uses a banded structure: increasing rates apply only to the portion of the purchase price within each band, not the entire price at the top rate — similar in principle to how Income Tax bands work.

Do first-time buyers pay less Stamp Duty?

Yes, typically — first-time buyers get a higher nil-rate threshold, but only up to a maximum purchase price ceiling. Above that ceiling, standard rates apply to the whole amount with no relief.

Does Stamp Duty apply in Scotland and Wales?

No — Scotland uses Land and Buildings Transaction Tax (LBTT) and Wales uses Land Transaction Tax (LTT), both separate systems with their own bands and rates, different from SDLT used in England and Northern Ireland.

Why is there an extra surcharge for a second property?

Buying an additional residential property (rather than your only or main home) generally attracts a surcharge on top of the standard SDLT bands, applied to the whole purchase price — this is a deliberate policy to moderate additional-property purchases.

A quick note

SDLT bands, thresholds and surcharges change over time and differ by buyer circumstance — this calculator gives an illustrative estimate using the standard England/NI structure. Always confirm the exact figure with your solicitor or gov.uk before exchanging contracts.
