KM to miles: the UK's split-measurement distance conversion

The UK runs an unusual dual system for distance: road signs, speed limits and speedometers use miles, while sport, science, weather and most international contexts use kilometres. That means "km to miles" gets searched constantly by UK residents dealing with European road trips, imported cars with kilometre speedometers, or race distances quoted in the metric figures used by the rest of the world.

One mile equals exactly 1.609344km — a fixed figure with no approximation, derived from the exact relationship between the inch and the centimetre. This calculator converts both ways: kilometres into miles, for reading a metric distance (from a European map, a race result, or an imported vehicle's dashboard) in the miles figure UK drivers actually think in, and miles back into kilometres for the reverse.

Conversion formula and method

The km-to-miles relationship is a single fixed division:

Miles = Kilometres ÷ 1.609344
Kilometres = Miles × 1.609344

Worked example: km to miles

Take 60km, a typical motorway journey segment. Divide by 1.609344: 60 ÷ 1.609344 = 37.282 miles. So 60km converts to 37.28 miles.

Worked example: miles to km

Take 70 miles, a UK motorway speed-limit-adjacent distance. Multiply by 1.609344: 70 × 1.609344 = 112.65km. This is the calculation the "Miles → KM" mode above runs automatically.

A quick mental shortcut

For a fast estimate, multiply kilometres by 0.62 (rather than the exact 0.621371) to get an approximate miles figure — accurate enough for a rough sense of distance, though always use the exact 1.609344 factor for anything precise like fuel calculations or pace times.

Historical context: an old English unit meets the metric system

The mile descends from the Roman "mille passus" (a thousand paces), but the modern English statute mile was fixed by an Act of Parliament under Elizabeth I in 1593 at exactly 5,280 feet — a length chosen to reconcile the Roman mile with the traditional English furlong (8 furlongs = 1 mile), rather than for any round-number convenience. The kilometre, by contrast, is a purely modern metric unit defined as one-thousandth of a metre when the metric system was formalised in the 1790s.

The exact modern relationship — 1 mile = 1.609344km precisely — follows directly from the 1959 international agreement that fixed 1 inch at exactly 2.54cm (5,280 feet × 12 inches/foot × 2.54cm/inch = 160,934.4cm = 1.609344km). The UK began converting road signage and official measurement toward metric from the 1960s, but famously stalled on the roads specifically — speed limits and distances on UK road signs remain in miles to this day, one of the most visible holdouts against full metrication anywhere in the country.

Where km to miles conversion actually gets used

  • Driving in Europe: UK drivers taking a car abroad need to read foreign speed limits and distances in kilometres and translate them into a familiar miles figure.
  • Imported and electric vehicles: some imported cars display speed and range in kilometres by default, prompting UK owners to convert for everyday use.
  • Running and cycling: most race distances (5km, 10km, half marathon, marathon) are defined in kilometres internationally, while UK runners often think and pace in miles.
  • Weather and geography: international news, maps and scientific data almost always use kilometres, requiring conversion for a UK audience used to miles for everyday distance.
  • Aviation and shipping context: while aviation itself typically uses nautical miles, general public discussion of flight and voyage distances often mixes kilometres (from international sources) with miles (from UK reporting), making a quick conversion useful for sanity-checking figures.
  • Fitness trackers and apps: some fitness apps and imported GPS devices default to kilometres, so UK users who think in miles for pace and distance often convert the displayed figures to check against personal targets.

The UK's continued use of miles on roads, decades after the rest of its official measurements went metric, means km-to-miles is one of the few conversions where a British audience needs it not because they're dealing with a foreign unit, but because two completely different systems are both still in everyday domestic use side by side.

Speed conversions: km/h to mph

The same 1.609344 factor governs speed as well as distance, since speed is just distance over time. To convert km/h to mph, divide by 1.609344 exactly as with a plain distance figure: 100km/h ÷ 1.609344 = 62.14mph, close to the UK's 70mph motorway limit but not identical to it — a common point of confusion for drivers of imported vehicles with km/h-only speedometers. A handful of reference points are worth knowing: 30mph (UK's typical urban limit) is 48.3km/h; 60mph (a common single-carriageway limit) is 96.6km/h; and 70mph (UK motorway limit) is 112.7km/h.

Common mistakes when converting km to miles

  • Using a rounded 1.6 instead of the exact 1.609344 factor. Fine for a rough mental estimate, but at longer distances (100km+) the rounding error compounds to a noticeable gap — use the precise factor for pace times, fuel calculations or anything measured.
  • Mixing up which direction to divide or multiply. Kilometres to miles is a division (÷1.609344); miles to kilometres is a multiplication (×1.609344) — reversing these produces a wildly incorrect result rather than just a rounding error.
  • Confusing the statute mile with the nautical mile. The nautical mile (1.852km, used at sea and in aviation) is a different, longer unit from the standard statute mile (1.609344km, used on roads) — always check which "mile" a source actually means.
  • Assuming speed and distance units always match. An imported car's speedometer showing km/h needs its readout converted to mph for UK speed limits — a separate but related conversion from simple distance.

Accuracy and precision note

Because 1 mile = 1.609344km is a fixed, internationally defined relationship rather than a measured approximation, every conversion on this page and in the calculator above is mathematically exact. Miles and kilometres are shown to three decimal places above for the precision that pace times and route planning often need — the underlying calculation carries full floating-point precision if an even finer figure is required.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert km to miles?

Divide the kilometre figure by 1.609344. For example, 10km ÷ 1.609344 = 6.214 miles. This factor is exact — it comes from the internationally defined mile of exactly 1,609.344 metres.

What is 10km in miles?

10km is 6.214 miles — one of the most commonly searched distance conversions, largely thanks to the popularity of the 10km running race distance.

How many miles is a 5k run?

A 5km run is 3.107 miles — commonly rounded to "about 3.1 miles" by runners.

How many miles is a marathon (42.195km)?

A marathon, officially 42.195km, is 26.22 miles — traditionally rounded to "26 miles 385 yards", the exact distance fixed for the 1908 London Olympics.

How do I convert miles to km?

Multiply the mile figure by 1.609344. For example, 10 miles × 1.609344 = 16.093km. This is the UK statute mile, the standard mile used for road distances.

What is 100km in miles?

100km is 62.137 miles — often rounded to "about 62 miles" for a quick road-trip or speed comparison.

Why is a mile 1.609344km and not a rounder number?

Because the mile (imperial/US customary) and the kilometre (metric) come from entirely independent historical measurement systems that were never designed to align on round numbers. The mile was fixed at exactly 5,280 feet by English statute centuries before the metric system existed, and 1.609344km is simply where that fixed imperial distance lands once converted using the exact foot-to-metre relationship.

How accurate is a km to miles conversion?

It's exact — 1 mile = 1.609344km is a fixed, internationally defined relationship (derived from 1 inch = 2.54cm exactly), so every km-to-miles conversion is precise arithmetic, not an estimate.

What is 21.1km (a half marathon) in miles?

A half marathon, officially 21.0975km, is 13.11 miles — half the full marathon distance, as the name suggests.

How many miles is 1km?

1km is 0.621 miles — a handy mental reference: roughly five-eighths of a mile.

What is 200km in miles?

200km is 124.27 miles — roughly a two-hour non-stop drive at UK motorway speed limits.

A quick note

All conversions above use the exact internationally defined relationship between the mile and the kilometre. For anything precision-critical (navigation, legal or commercial use), always confirm against the source figures rather than a rounded display value.
