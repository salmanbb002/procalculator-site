Sleep Cycle Calculator

Find the best bedtime or wake time based on 90-minute sleep cycles.

What is the Sleep Cycle Calculator?

A sleep cycle calculator suggests the best bedtime or wake-up time based on the theory that waking up between sleep cycles (rather than mid-cycle) leaves you feeling more refreshed. It works by treating sleep as a series of roughly 90-minute cycles, then either counting backwards from a wake-up time in 90-minute blocks (plus the time it typically takes you to fall asleep) to suggest bedtimes, or counting forwards from a bedtime to suggest wake times, offering options for 3, 4, 5 or 6 full cycles. To work it out manually, subtract (or add) multiples of 90 minutes from your target time, plus your typical time to fall asleep — individual cycle length actually varies from roughly 70 to 120 minutes, so treat the result as a helpful guide rather than an exact science.

How it works

Sleep happens in cycles lasting roughly 90 minutes each, moving through lighter and deeper stages including REM sleep — waking up in the middle of a cycle (particularly deep sleep) tends to feel much groggier than waking at the boundary between cycles. This calculator works backwards or forwards from your target wake or bed time in 90-minute blocks, adding a typical allowance for the time it takes to actually fall asleep.

UK context

This is a popular sleep-planning approach, but it's a useful approximation rather than a precise science — actual cycle length varies from person to person and even night to night, commonly somewhere in a 70-120 minute range rather than being exactly 90 minutes for everyone.

Tips

  • Most adults need somewhere around 4-6 full cycles (roughly 6-9 hours) for good-quality rest, though individual sleep needs genuinely vary.
  • Consistency in your sleep and wake times, even more than hitting an exact cycle boundary, is one of the most well-supported factors for sleep quality.
  • If you consistently wake up feeling groggy despite following the cycle timing, it may be worth looking at overall sleep quality and duration rather than just timing.

Frequently asked questions

Is the 90-minute sleep cycle exact for everyone?

No — it's a commonly used average, but actual cycle length varies between individuals and even night to night, typically somewhere in a 70-120 minute range.

Why does waking mid-cycle feel worse than waking between cycles?

Cycles move through lighter and deeper sleep stages, including REM — being woken during deep sleep tends to cause more grogginess (sleep inertia) than waking during a lighter stage near a cycle boundary.

A quick note

Based on the standard ~90-minute sleep cycle model, a helpful guideline rather than an exact science — individual cycle length varies.
