Wedding Cost vs House Deposit

Compare the average UK wedding cost to a house deposit.

What is the Wedding Cost vs House Deposit?

A wedding cost vs house deposit calculator gives a perspective comparison between your own wedding budget and your target house deposit, expressed as a percentage, using figures you provide rather than a fabricated national average. It works by simply dividing your wedding budget by your target deposit and multiplying by 100 to show what percentage of the deposit the wedding spend represents, alongside the plain cash difference between the two. To calculate it manually: wedding budget ÷ target deposit × 100 = percentage — both average wedding costs and average house deposits vary hugely by location and personal choices, so this tool deliberately uses your own real or planned figures rather than presenting an invented "average" as fact.

How it works

This compares your wedding budget against your target house deposit as a percentage, giving a quick perspective check on how the two figures relate — simple division, but a genuinely useful reframing for a decision many couples weigh up directly against each other.

UK context

Both wedding costs and house deposits vary enormously depending on location, choices and circumstances — there's no single "average" figure for either that would be meaningful for a specific couple's actual planning, which is exactly why this tool asks for your own real or planned numbers rather than assuming a national average for either.

Tips

  • Use your actual planned wedding budget and a realistic target deposit for your local area (deposit requirements vary a lot by region and property price) for the most useful comparison.
  • This comparison can be a genuinely useful conversation-starter for couples weighing up priorities between a wedding and house-buying timeline — not just a fun statistic.

Frequently asked questions

Does this use average UK wedding and house prices?

No — both wedding costs and house deposits vary too much by region and individual choices for a single national average to be meaningful. Enter your own actual or planned figures for a comparison that's relevant to you.

Is this meant to suggest weddings aren't worth the cost?

Not at all — it's simply a perspective tool for comparing two numbers that many couples genuinely do weigh up against each other when planning finances, without making a judgement either way.

A quick note

Enter your own actual or planned figures — not based on a fabricated "average" wedding or house price, since both vary hugely by location and choices.
