KG to lbs: converting between the world's two most common weight units

Kilograms and pounds are the two units that between them cover almost every weight figure you'll encounter day to day — the kilogram as the metric, scientific and internationally standard unit, and the pound as the everyday unit still used for body weight and general goods in the United States, and alongside stone for body weight in the UK. One kilogram equals exactly 2.2046226218 pounds, a fixed, non-approximate figure defined by international agreement — so a kg-to-lbs conversion is precise arithmetic, never a rough estimate.

This calculator handles the conversion both ways: kilograms into pounds, for reading a metric weight (from scales, a product spec, or a European or international source) in the pound figure you might recognise more easily, and pounds back into kilograms, for the reverse — useful for US recipes, US-sourced fitness data, or converting a pounds figure for a UK form or record that expects kilograms.

Conversion formula and method

The kg-to-lbs relationship is a single fixed multiplication, exact to as many decimal places as you need:

Pounds = Kilograms × 2.2046226218
Kilograms = Pounds × 0.45359237

Worked example: kg to lbs

Take 68kg. Multiply by 2.2046226218: 68 × 2.2046226218 = 149.91lb. So 68kg converts to 149.91lb — or, in UK terms, 10 stone 9.9lb.

Worked example: lbs to kg

Take 180lb. Multiply by 0.45359237: 180 × 0.45359237 = 81.65kg. So 180lb converts to 81.65kg.

Converting via stone

If you need the UK stone-and-pounds format rather than a raw decimal, divide the pounds figure by 14 as a second step — or use our dedicated KG to Stone calculator or Pounds to Stone calculator, which do the extra step for you.

Historical context: two systems, one fixed bridge

The kilogram is the base unit of mass in the International System of Units (SI), formalised in 1889 and, since 2019, defined not by a physical object but by fixing the value of the Planck constant — an abstraction that has zero practical effect on everyday conversions. The pound, in its modern "avoirdupois pound" form, descends from English trade custom and was used, with regional variation, for centuries before standardisation.

The two units were finally locked together in 1959 through the International Yard and Pound Agreement, signed by the UK, the US, Australia, Canada, New Zealand and South Africa, which fixed the pound at exactly 0.45359237kg. Before that agreement, slightly different pound definitions existed in different countries, meaning a "pound" in one place wasn't always bit-for-bit identical to a "pound" elsewhere. Since 1959, there's been no ambiguity — every kg-to-lbs conversion anywhere in the world uses the same exact factor.

Where kg to lbs conversion actually gets used

  • Travel and luggage: airlines publish baggage limits in kilograms internationally, but many US passengers and US-based tools think and calculate in pounds.
  • Fitness and strength training: gym equipment, competition results and international programming are usually in kilograms, while some UK and US commentary defaults to pounds.
  • Shipping and logistics: parcel weight limits and courier pricing are sometimes quoted in kg by international carriers and need converting for pound-based invoicing or comparison.
  • Recipes and product labelling: US recipes and packaging are typically in pounds and ounces, while UK and EU equivalents are in kilograms and grams.
  • Academic and scientific work: research papers, lab equipment specs and international standards are written in kilograms almost universally, so US-based readers and collaborators frequently need a quick pounds equivalent to build an intuitive sense of scale.
  • Online marketplaces and imports: goods listed on European or international sites often show weight in kilograms, which UK and US buyers estimating postage or handling costs may want translated into a more familiar pounds figure.

In practice, kg-to-lbs is one of the highest-frequency conversions on the web precisely because it sits at the intersection of two entire measurement systems rather than a single specialist niche — anyone reading international content involving weight eventually runs into it.

Common mistakes when converting kg to lbs

  • Rounding the factor too early. Using 2.2 instead of 2.2046226218 is fine for a rough mental estimate, but at larger weights (100kg+) the rounding error compounds to a full pound or more — use the precise factor for anything that matters.
  • Confusing pounds with UK stone-and-pounds format. "154 pounds" and "11 stone 0lb" are the same weight, but a raw decimal-pounds figure is not the same thing as a stone-and-pounds figure — check which format a source actually means.
  • Mixing up avoirdupois and troy pounds. The everyday pound (avoirdupois, 0.45359237kg) is different from the historic troy pound (used for precious metals, 0.3732417kg) — for body weight, luggage, and general goods, it's always the avoirdupois pound.
  • Assuming ounces here mean fluid ounces. The ounces shown on this page are weight (avoirdupois) ounces — 16 to a pound — not fluid ounces, which measure volume.

Accuracy and precision note

Because 1 pound = 0.45359237kg is an internationally fixed, exact definition (not a measured approximation), every conversion on this page and in the calculator above is mathematically exact. The only rounding happens in how many decimal places are displayed — pounds are shown to two decimal places above for everyday precision, and the underlying calculation carries full floating-point precision if you need a more exact figure for a specific use case.

For casual, everyday use — checking a parcel weight or getting a rough sense of a body-weight figure — rounding to one decimal place or even the nearest whole pound is perfectly adequate. It's only in medical dosing, commercial trade, engineering tolerances or scientific reporting that the extra decimal places genuinely matter, and in those contexts the exact 2.2046226218 factor (or its inverse, 0.45359237) should always be used rather than a rounded shortcut like "×2.2".

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert kg to lbs?

Multiply the kilogram figure by 2.2046226218. For example, 70kg × 2.2046226218 = 154.32lb. This factor is exact — it comes directly from the internationally defined relationship 1lb = 0.45359237kg, inverted.

What is 70kg in lbs?

70kg converts to 154.32lb — or 11 stone 0.3lb in UK stone-and-pounds terms.

What is 1kg in lbs?

1kg is 2.2046226218lb, almost always rounded to 2.2lb in everyday use.

How do I convert lbs to kg?

Multiply the pounds figure by 0.45359237. For example, 154lb × 0.45359237 = 69.85kg. This is the exact, legally defined conversion factor between the two units.

What is 100kg in pounds?

100kg is 220.46lb — a figure that comes up often as a round psychological milestone in fitness and strength-training contexts.

Why isn't 1kg exactly 2 or 2.5 pounds?

Because the kilogram (metric) and the pound (imperial/US customary) come from two completely independent historical measurement systems that were never designed to align on round numbers. The 2.20462 factor is simply where the two systems happen to intersect.

Is a kilogram heavier than a pound?

Yes — a kilogram is about 2.2 times heavier than a pound. One kilogram equals roughly 2.2 pounds, so a pound is the much smaller unit of the two.

How accurate is a kg to lbs conversion?

It's exact, not approximate. Since 1959, the pound has been legally defined as precisely 0.45359237kg by international agreement, so every kg-to-lbs conversion is exact arithmetic, not an estimate — any rounding you see is purely in the displayed decimal places.

What is 50kg in pounds?

50kg is 110.23lb — just over the 110lb mark, commonly referenced for parcel and luggage weight limits as well as body weight.

How many pounds is half a kilogram?

0.5kg is 1.10lb — exactly half of the 1kg = 2.2046226218lb figure.

What is 1000kg (a tonne) in pounds?

1,000kg (one metric tonne) is 2,204.62lb. This is roughly, but not exactly, equal to a US "short ton" (2,000lb) or a UK "long ton" (2,240lb) — a metric tonne sits between the two.

A quick note

All conversions above use the exact internationally defined relationship between the kilogram and the pound. For anything precision-critical (medical, legal or commercial use), always confirm against the source figures rather than a rounded display value.
