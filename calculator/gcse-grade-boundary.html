GCSE Grade Boundary Checker

Estimate a 9-1 GCSE grade from a raw exam mark.

How it works

This tool converts a raw mark into a percentage of the total available marks, then maps that percentage onto evenly spaced illustrative bands (90%+ for grade 9, down to below 10% for grade U) purely to give a rough sense of where a mark sits — it deliberately does not claim to show your real grade boundary.

UK context

This is an important distinction worth being explicit about: real GCSE grade boundaries are set separately for every individual subject, every exam board, and every exam series (each sitting), based on how that specific paper performed across all candidates that year — a mark that scores a grade 7 in one subject or series can require a meaningfully different percentage than the same grade in a different subject or year. There's no fixed universal mark-to-grade mapping that applies across all GCSEs, which is exactly why exam boards publish official grade boundaries separately after every results day, not in advance.

Tips

  • For your actual grade, always check your specific exam board's published grade boundaries for that exact subject and exam series (available on the exam board's website after results day) — this tool is illustrative only, not a substitute for that.
  • Grade boundaries can shift meaningfully year to year for the same subject as exam boards adjust for paper difficulty, which is exactly why a fixed universal percentage-to-grade table can't be accurate.

Frequently asked questions

Is this tool's grade the same as my real GCSE grade?

No — this uses evenly spaced illustrative bands to give a rough sense of where a mark sits, not real grade boundaries. Actual boundaries are set individually per subject, exam board and exam series, and are only published officially after results day.

Why don't GCSE grade boundaries stay the same every year?

Exam boards set boundaries after each exam series based on how that specific paper performed across all candidates, to keep the difficulty of achieving a given grade broadly consistent year to year even when the paper itself is harder or easier — this means boundaries genuinely shift, sometimes noticeably, between years.
