University Degree Classification

Estimate your final degree classification from module marks.

What is the University Degree Classification?

A university degree classification calculator estimates your final honours classification from your average marks across different years of study, weighted according to how much each year counts toward the final result. It works by multiplying each year's average percentage by its weighting, summing those weighted figures and dividing by the total weighting to get an overall weighted average, then comparing that average against the standard UK classification bands: 70%+ for a First, 60-69% for a 2:1, 50-59% for a 2:2 and 40-49% for a Third. To calculate it manually: (Year 2 average × Year 2 weight + Year 3 average × Year 3 weight) ÷ (Year 2 weight + Year 3 weight) — exact weighting schemes, borderline rules and whether Year 1 counts vary significantly by university, so check your specific institution's regulations.

How it works

Most UK undergraduate degrees combine marks from your later years (commonly year 2 and year 3, sometimes with different weightings) into a single weighted average, which is then mapped onto the standard classification bands. This calculator applies your entered weightings to your year averages and checks the result against those standard bands.

UK context

The standard UK classification bands are First (70%+), Upper Second/2:1 (60-69%), Lower Second/2:2 (50-59%) and Third (40-49%), but the exact weighting scheme — how much year 2 counts versus year 3, whether year 1 counts at all, and any borderline or discretionary uplift rules — varies significantly between universities and even between courses at the same university.

Tips

  • Check your specific university's exam regulations (often published in a student handbook or on the intranet) for your course's exact weighting scheme, since it directly affects your final classification.
  • Many universities have a "borderline" or discretionary rule that can round a close result up to the next classification, based on your marks profile — this calculator applies a strict cut-off, so a real result close to a boundary might come out slightly higher.

Frequently asked questions

What are the standard UK degree classification bands?

First-Class Honours (70%+), Upper Second-Class Honours/2:1 (60-69%), Lower Second-Class Honours/2:2 (50-59%), and Third-Class Honours (40-49%) are the standard bands used by most UK universities.

Does year 1 count towards my final degree classification?

This varies by university and course — many UK degrees don't count year 1 towards the final classification at all (though you still need to pass it), while others weight it lightly. Check your specific institution's regulations.
