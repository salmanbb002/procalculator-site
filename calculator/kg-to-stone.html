KG to stone: converting the metric weight into the UK's everyday unit

The kilogram is the legal, scientific and medical unit for weight in the UK — it's what appears on hospital and GP records, on official documents, and on food and product labelling. But when people in the UK talk about their own body weight out loud, they usually still say it in stone and pounds. That gap is exactly why "kg to stone" is one of the most consistently searched weight conversions in the country: someone gets a number in kilograms from a set of scales, an app, a doctor, or a product spec, and needs it translated into the figure they'd actually recognise or quote.

Unlike the pounds-to-stone conversion, which is a clean division by 14, a kilograms-to-stone conversion involves two steps — kilograms to pounds, then pounds to stone — because the kilogram and the stone come from entirely different measurement systems (metric and imperial) that were never designed to align neatly. That's also why kg-to-stone searches so often include decimals, like 63.5kg, 82.6kg or 95.3kg: real-world kilogram weights from scales and medical records are rarely round numbers, and this calculator is built to handle that decimal input cleanly rather than forcing whole numbers.

Conversion formula and method

Converting kilograms to stone is a two-step calculation:

Pounds = Kilograms × 2.20462
Stone = Pounds ÷ 14

Worked example with a decimal input

Take 63.5kg — a very commonly searched figure. First convert to pounds: 63.5 × 2.20462 = 140.0lb. Then divide by 14: 140 ÷ 14 = 10 exactly. So 63.5kg is precisely 10 stone 0lb — no rounding needed, which is part of why this particular figure gets searched so often.

Now take a less tidy example: 82.6kg. Convert to pounds: 82.6 × 2.20462 = 182.1lb. Divide by 14: 182.1 ÷ 14 = 13.01, so the stone figure is 13, and the remaining pounds are 182.1 − (13 × 14) = 182.1 − 182 = 0.1lb. Result: 82.6kg is 13 stone 0.1lb.

Going the other way: stone to kg

To reverse the conversion, multiply the stone figure by 14 to get pounds, then multiply by 0.45359237 to get kilograms. For pounds-only conversions in either direction, use our dedicated Pounds to Stone calculator, which also handles the reverse "stone and pounds → lb" direction directly.

Body weight in context: real-world reference points

Kilogram figures can be harder to picture intuitively if you're used to thinking in stone, so here's how some common kg values line up with recognisable UK reference points:

  • 63.5kg (10st 0lb) — a frequently cited "average adult female" reference weight in UK health resources, and a common target figure in weight-management contexts.
  • 70kg (11st 0.3lb) — very close to the average UK adult male body weight reported in national health survey data.
  • 80kg (12st 8.4lb) — around the upper-normal to overweight BMI boundary for an average UK adult male height, depending on build.
  • 90kg (14st 2.4lb) — a figure commonly discussed in rugby and strength-sport contexts, sitting around typical amateur front-row forward body weight.
  • 100kg (15st 10.5lb) — a widely recognised "three-figure" milestone weight that comes up often in fitness and health articles as a round psychological marker.

Historical context: two systems that were never designed to match

The kilogram is a product of the metric system introduced in France in the 1790s and formalised internationally through the Metre Convention of 1875, designed from first principles around powers of ten. The stone, by contrast, descends from British and Irish trade custom — originally the weight of an actual stone used on balance scales, standardised at 14 pounds avoirdupois by the Weights and Measures Act 1835. Because these two systems evolved independently, centuries apart, on different continents, there was never any attempt to make round numbers convert neatly between them — which is exactly why 1 stone works out to an untidy 6.35029kg rather than a clean 6kg or 6.5kg.

The UK began its shift toward the metric system in the 1960s, with kilograms formally becoming the primary legal unit for most trade and scientific purposes over the following decades — a process defined in law through instruments including the Weights and Measures Act 1985. Medicine, in particular, moved to kilograms across UK clinical practice for dosing accuracy and international consistency. Body weight in everyday speech, however, stayed with stone — leaving the two systems to coexist, which is the reason a "kg to stone" converter remains genuinely useful rather than a historical curiosity.

Where kg-to-stone conversion actually gets used

  • Medical and fitness apps: many international apps and smart scales report weight in kilograms by default, which UK users often want translated into stone to match how they think about their own weight.
  • Travel and immigration: weight figures on visa medicals, sports federations, or documents from outside the UK are usually in kilograms and sometimes need converting for UK-facing forms or conversations.
  • Parcels and luggage: airlines and couriers state weight limits in kilograms, which some UK users find easier to sanity-check once converted to a stone-and-pounds figure they recognise.
  • Sport and strength training: gym equipment and international competition results are usually in kilograms, while UK commentary and everyday conversation about an athlete's weight often reverts to stone.

Common mistakes when converting kg to stone

  • Skipping the pounds step and dividing kg directly by 6.35. While mathematically similar (1 stone ≈ 6.35029kg), dividing kg by 6.35 directly and then trying to split the remainder into pounds afterwards is a common source of small errors — it's more reliable to convert to pounds first, then divide by 14.
  • Confusing decimal stone with a stone-and-pounds figure. "12.6 stone" is not "12 stone 6lb" — the .6 is six-tenths of a stone, which works out to about 8.4lb, so 12.6 stone is actually 12 stone 8.4lb.
  • Rounding the kilogram figure before converting. If your original weight is something like 82.6kg, convert using the full decimal rather than rounding to 83kg first — at typical body-weight ranges, early rounding can shift the final stone-and-pounds answer by a noticeable fraction of a pound.
  • Assuming international apps and devices use stone. Most smart scales, fitness trackers and health apps built outside the UK report weight in kilograms or pounds only — if you want a stone figure, you'll usually need to convert it yourself, as this calculator does.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert kg to stone?

Multiply the kilogram figure by 2.20462 to get pounds, then divide by 14 to get stone. The whole-number part is the stone; multiplying the decimal remainder by 14 gives the leftover pounds. For example, 70kg × 2.20462 = 154.3lb, and 154.3 ÷ 14 = 11.02, so 70kg is 11 stone 0.3lb.

What is 70kg in stone?

70kg converts to 11 stone 0.3lb (11.02 stone as a decimal), or 154.3 pounds.

What is 63.5kg in stone?

63.5kg is exactly 10 stone 0lb (10.00 stone) — it's a commonly searched figure because it lands almost precisely on a round stone value, equal to 140 pounds.

What is 80kg in stone and pounds?

80kg is 12 stone 8.4lb, or 12.60 stone as a decimal — approximately 176.4 pounds.

How many kg is a stone?

One stone is approximately 6.35029 kilograms (14 pounds × 0.45359237kg per pound). It's not a round number in kilograms because the stone and kilogram come from two different measurement systems that don't align neatly.

What is 100kg in stone?

100kg converts to 15 stone 10.5lb, or 15.75 stone as a decimal — about 220.5 pounds.

Why do UK weight conversions mix kilograms and stone?

Medical records, scientific contexts and most retail weighing in the UK use kilograms as the legal metric standard, while stone remains the everyday spoken unit for body weight — so converting between the two is a routine, genuine need rather than a niche one.

How accurate is a kg to stone conversion?

It's exact to as many decimal places as you display, since both the kilogram-to-pound and pound-to-stone relationships are fixed, defined values with no approximation involved. Any imprecision comes only from rounding the displayed figure, not the underlying calculation.

What is 82.6kg in stone?

82.6kg is 13 stone 0.1lb, or 13.01 stone as a decimal — about 182.1 pounds.

What is 10 kilos in stone?

10kg converts to 1 stone 8lb, or 1.57 stone as a decimal — about 22 pounds. This size of conversion comes up often for luggage and parcel weights as well as body weight.

A quick note

All conversions above use the exact legal definitions of the kilogram, pound and stone. For anything precision-critical (medical, legal or commercial use), always confirm against the source figures rather than a rounded display value.
